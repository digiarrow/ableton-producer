# Stage 3: Hook

Load with `_common.md` and `best_practices.md`. The hook is what Digi Arrow judges the track by (taste log: Mariô was rejected mostly on sound and hook). Spend the most time here.

## One hook
Groove first, then **one** simple, repetitive, memorable element that gives DJs a crowd reaction point (`../creative_direction.md`). For an original tech house track that is usually a **short spoken or chopped vocal hook** or a **synth stab**. One strong hook beats a crowded arrangement: anything added later supports it and stays out of its register. In a remake or reference study, no vocals or vocal-like layers: use a stab, lead or pluck.

## Choose the type
- **Vocal chop** (Guetta, Don't Blink): short shouts or syllables from a free, cleared vocal pack (`downloads.md`), pitched to the key, 2 to 4 hits per bar in fixed 16th slots.
- **Stab** (tech house): short chord or single-note stab, saw or organ, 1/16 to 1/8 long.
- **Lead or riff** (Prodigy, big room): distorted or brassy mono line, 1 to 2 bar call and response.
- **Pluck or arp** (Prydz, melodic): 16th arp with filter automation, delay for space.

## Steps
1. Listen through the reference's hook in a solo capture section: count hits per 4 bars, note the register (e.g. around A4), length and brightness (2 to 6 kHz).
2. Pick 2 to 4 candidate sounds (palette first, then Vital/Surge presets, then free vocal or one-shot packs). Capture each playing the same 2-bar idea silently and compare high-mid and centroid with the reference hook section.
3. Write an **original** motif (never copy the reference melody): 2 or 4 bars, rhythm first, 3 to 5 notes, repetition with one change at the end of the phrase. Leave space; the hook answers the bass, not doubles it.
4. Variation every 8 bars: stutter, octave, filter or a dropped note.
5. Space: send to delay (dotted 1/8) and reverb returns; high-pass the hook at 150 to 250 Hz. Keep tails short in the drop (low pre-delay, short plate); long tails only in breaks.
6. **Hook alone test**: the hook with only the kick must already work. If it needs pads, layers or FX to sound good, change the sound or the notes, don't add parts.
7. **Own its slot**: the hook owns 500 Hz to 4 kHz in one octave; anything else melodic in the drop sits in another octave or only in the hook's gaps. No second lead.
8. **Uplift** (v02 "not uplifting"): prefer a rising shape, phrase endings on the 3rd or 5th above the root, a bright sound (2 to 6 kHz presence). Tease it filtered in the break, full on the drop.

## Done when
Digi Arrow rates the hook loop. If under 6, try a different sound before rewriting notes.
