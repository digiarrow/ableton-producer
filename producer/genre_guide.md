# Genre guide (approximate, adjust to the references)

| Genre | Typical tempo (BPM) | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| House | 120 to 126 | 8-bar phrases; 16 to 32-bar DJ intro; piano, vocal or chord hooks |
| Deep house | 118 to 124 | 8 to 16-bar phrases; warm chords; subtle changes |
| Afro house | 118 to 124 | 16 to 32-bar phrases; percussion-led; long builds |
| Tech house | 124 to 128 | 8-bar phrases; 32-bar DJ intro and outro; break and build into a 32-bar drop |
| Progressive house / techno | 120 to 128 | Long 16 to 32-bar phrases; slow builds; long breakdowns |
| Melodic techno | 120 to 126 | 16 to 32-bar phrases; long breakdown with an arp or lead |
| Techno | 125 to 140 | 8 to 32-bar phrases; long DJ intros; changes through layering and automation |
| Trance | 135 to 140 | 16 to 32-bar phrases; long breakdown with melody; big drop |
| Drum and bass | 170 to 176 | 16 to 32-bar phrases; long DJ intro; two drops |
| Big room / EDM | 126 to 130 | 8 to 16-bar phrases; build and drop; shorter intros |
| Hip hop / pop | varies | Verse, chorus, bridge; 4 to 8-bar sections; no long DJ intro |

Pick the tempo inside the range that suits the track, or go outside it when the references do. There is no fixed BPM.

Key: pick the key from the references (analyze.py reports it) or what suits the bass: for club tracks, a bass root between F and A keeps the sub fundamental around 43 to 55 Hz, where club systems hit hardest. Note the Camelot code in the song sheet for DJ mixing.

## Reference artists, what to take from each
- **Don't Blink:** punchy modern tech house, bouncy bass, vocal-chop hooks, short tight drops.
- **Carl Cox:** driving percussive techno, rolling toms and loops, long DJ tools.
- **David Guetta:** big hooks, clear build and drop, bright mixes.
- **The Prodigy:** aggressive breaks, distorted hooks and bass, attitude.
- **Eric Prydz:** long progressive builds, wide evolving pads, filter journeys.
- **Marco Carola:** minimal rolling grooves, few elements, small changes over long phrases.
